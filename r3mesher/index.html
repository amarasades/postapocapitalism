R3mesher

Remesher // for 3D garments

Turn mess into mesh, so things get made and repaired instead of binned.

R3mesher takes a garment from CLO3D, or any GLB or OBJ, and gives back a clean, evenly sized triangle or quad mesh at whatever density you ask for, without breaking the texture. It's a desktop app, a command-line tool and a library, all running the same engine.

Also written r3mesher or R3 Mesher. Part of the postapocapitalism software family, with Sc3ne and Cad3t.

What it does

06 functions
01 // Density

Any density you ask for

A ratio, a face count or an edge length. A quarter of the triangles, about 20,000, 1 cm edges, or the same count with better-shaped triangles. Tight folds get smaller triangles, flat panels larger ones.

02 // Texture

The texture survives

UV seams are walls the mesh can't cross, so no triangle straddles one. UVs, normals, tangents and colours are read back from the original surface under every new corner. Materials and everything else in the file pass through untouched.

03 // Cloth

It knows how clothes are built

Thick cloth stays a closed shell, front and back matching vertex for vertex. Welts, zipper tape, piping and pockets stay on their side of the panel. Buttons, rivets and zipper pulls are left exactly as they are.

04 // Quads

Quads that follow the pattern

All-quad or quad-dominant output. On CLO's flat-pattern UVs the quads are laid as a square grid along each piece's edges, so they run with the fabric's grain. Written as OBJ, which Blender, Maya, Marvelous and CLO all open.

05 // Bake

Detail kept in the maps

Bake the original's folds and wrinkles into a normal map on the light mesh. Bake topstitching, often a third of a CLO export's faces, into the colour and normal maps instead of carrying the thread geometry.

06 // Measure

Numbers, not vibes

Every job reports edge-length evenness, smallest angles, sliver triangles and how far the result strays from the original. In the app, drag a split across the model to compare before and after.

How it works

Drop // pick // compare // export
  1. Drop a garment inA GLB or OBJ from CLO3D, or a .sc3g package from Sc3ne.
  2. Pick a densityTriangles, quads or mostly quads; per piece if you want, and pick parts to keep exactly as they came.
  3. CompareDrag the split between the original and the result, check the quality numbers.
  4. ExportGLB or OBJ, with baked maps if you asked for them, tuned for Blender, Unreal or the web.

Questions

What is a remesher?

A remesher rebuilds a 3D model's surface out of new, well-shaped triangles or quads, at the density you need, while keeping its shape. Simulation exports like CLO3D's are dense and uneven; a remeshed garment is lighter to render, simulate, edit and send.

Does it only work with CLO3D?

It's built around CLO3D garments, but any GLB or OBJ goes in. The garment-specific parts (shells, layers, trims, topstitching) simply have nothing to do on other models.

Will it break my UVs?

No. UV seams are hard constraints, and every attribute is transferred from the original surface at the end. If the UVs can't hold a baked map (CLO's often overlap), a second, clean UV set is made for the bake and the originals stay.

How do you spell it?

R3mesher, with a 3 for the e. r3mesher and R3 Mesher are the same tool.
